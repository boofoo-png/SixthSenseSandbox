Discovery Notes: Marketing Dashboard

Pain Points

Businesses may need to switch between multiple marketing platforms to manage their content and track performance.

Creating and scheduling content across different social media platforms can be time-consuming.

Analyzing competitors and identifying marketing trends can be difficult.

Marketing data can be overwhelming and difficult to understand.

Businesses may struggle to identify which marketing strategies are working and which need improvement.

Success Metrics (KPIs)

Users can create and schedule content through one dashboard.

Users can view marketing performance through clear charts and analytics.

Users can compare competitors and identify trends.

Users can easily navigate between the dashboard's main features.

Users can connect their business social media accounts securely.

The dashboard works on desktop and mobile browsers.

Constraints

Timeline: To be determined based on the project deadline.

Budget: To be determined based on available resources.

Technical: Web-based marketing dashboard that will eventually connect to client social media accounts through supported APIs.

Development: Build a working prototype first, followed by a functional dashboard.

Data: The prototype may use sample data before real social media accounts are connected.

Scope: Focus on content creation, scheduling, analytics, and competitor analysis.

Competitors

Hootsuite: Scheduling-first platform with AI post and image generation and built-in competitor benchmarking. From $99/user/month; 14-day free trial.

Sprout Social: Publishing, unified inbox, and analytics for teams; its strongest analytics and social listening are paid add-ons. From $79/month; 30-day free trial.

Metricool: Low-cost all-in-one tool covering creation, scheduling, analytics, and up to 100 competitors. Free plan; paid from $20/month. Closest match to our scope.

Keyhole: Social listening, hashtag tracking, and sentiment analysis; no content creation or scheduling. Acquired by Muck Rack (PR software) in August 2024 and now sold only through Muck Rack sales demos.

Predis.ai: AI content generator for videos, carousels, captions, and hashtags, with scheduling and light competitor tracking; very limited analytics. From $24/month; 3-day free trial.

Positioning: Each competitor is strongest in one or two areas. Our dashboard aims to combine content creation, scheduling, analytics, and competitor analysis in one simple, easy-to-understand tool.

Gaps and Opportunities

1. AI content and analytics in one affordable tool. Predis.ai has strong AI content but very limited analytics; Metricool has strong analytics but limited AI credits. Hootsuite and Sprout Social offer both but start at $79-$99/month. Pain points: switching between platforms; creating and scheduling content is time-consuming.

2. Plain-language insights. Competitors present analytics as metrics, charts, and reports, and the more useful versions (custom reports, exports) sit on higher tiers or add-ons. An opportunity is to explain results in simple terms, such as which posts worked and what to try next. Pain points: data is overwhelming; hard to tell which strategies are working.

3. Affordable competitor and trend tracking. Trend tracking is only on Hootsuite Professional ($199/month), Keyhole (sales-only, enterprise pricing), or partially on Predis.ai. Metricool tracks up to 100 competitors cheaply but does not list trend tracking. Pain point: analyzing competitors and identifying trends is difficult.

4. Sentiment and social listening without enterprise pricing. Listening and sentiment are paid add-ons on Sprout Social, higher-tier on Hootsuite, and sales-only on Keyhole. Metricool and Predis.ai do not list them. Pain point: analyzing competitors and identifying trends is difficult.

5. Simple, predictable pricing for small businesses. Hootsuite charges per user, Sprout Social puts key features in add-ons, and Keyhole has no self-serve signup. Pain point: switching between multiple platforms (and paying for each).

6. Approval workflows. Only Metricool lists approvals, and only on its Advanced plan. This is not linked to an existing pain point; validate with users before building it.

Research Review

The comparison sharpens the three research questions in USER_RESEARCH.md:

Specific: instead of asking users to choose between four broad areas, ask them to rank specific features from the comparison table (for example AI captions, best time to post, competitor benchmarking, plain-language insights).

Practical: gaps 1 and 5 suggest the main difficulty may be paying for and switching between separate tools; confirm this with users.

Actionable: price, add-on costs, and per-user pricing are likely reasons to switch; ask about them directly.

Questions to consider adding to user research:

Which marketing tool do you use now, which plan are you on, and roughly how much do you pay per month?

Which social networks do you post to most often?

Would short plain-language summaries (for example "what worked this week") be more useful to you than charts?

How do you track competitors today, and how many competitors do you follow?

Do you use AI to write posts or captions, and do you trust the results?

Does anyone need to approve your posts before they are published?
